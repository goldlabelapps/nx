> [CleverText text="What has become critical is experience"]  

As a developer, the question is now; how fast can you despecialise, solve the whole problem, and deliver using industry-standard patterns?

Before AI-assisted development, scale needed specialisation. You hired a frontend person, a backend person, a DevOps person, a designer, maybe a data person, then spent time coordinating all of them. That made sense when switching disciplines was expensive.

If you were a React developer, you stayed a React developer. If you wanted to expand into another stack, it took months or years to become truly useful.

Despecialisation is the ability to move across disciplines quickly, without losing quality.

Not pretending to know everything.
Not being sloppy.
Not vibe coding your way into production disasters

It means operating as a High-Trust Solo Technical Operator who can

- define the problem
- choose pragmatic architecture
- ship frontend and backend work
- design clean UX where needed
- handle deployment and reliability basics
- use AI as force-multiplier, not a crutch

#### What changed?

AI dramatically lowered the switching cost between domains. The bottleneck is no longer only "Can you write this syntax?". Instead;

- judgment
- systems thinking
- pattern recognition
- verification discipline
- communication

In short, what has become critical is experience
